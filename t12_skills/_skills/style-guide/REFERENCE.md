# Style Guide Reference

## Brand Voice at a Glance

| Attribute        | Do                                              | Don't                                           |
|------------------|-------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Voice            | Clear, confident, warm                          | Vague, overly formal, or salesy                 |
| Tone             | Match the content type (see below)              | Use one tone for every message                  |
| Sentences        | Short, 15 to 20 words on average                | Long chains joined with many commas             |
| Vocabulary       | Plain words that the reader uses every day      | Jargon such as "leverage", "synergy", "utilize" |
| Point of view    | "we" for the company, "you" for the reader      | "the customer", "the user", "one"               |
| Contractions     | Yes in support and social content               | Avoid in formal emails and legal notices        |

## Content Types

### Emails

- **Subject lines:** 40 characters or less. Say what the reader gets or must do. Avoid "Important!!!", "Newsletter #12", and all caps.
- **Body:** one purpose per email. Start with the reason in the first sentence. Put one call to action (CTA) in bold near the end, for example **Confirm your booking**. Close with a short sign-off, such as "Thanks, The Support Team".
- **Tone dial:**
  - Transactional (receipts, confirmations): factual, short, no marketing language.
  - Marketing (offers, news): friendly and specific, one benefit per paragraph.
  - Support (problems, complaints): empathetic first, then the solution.

### Blog Posts

- **Structure:** one H1 title, H2 sections, H3 only for details. The intro is 2 to 3 sentences and says what the reader learns.
- **Style:** reading level around grade 8. Spell out an acronym the first time it appears.
- **Headings:** sentence case. Make them outcome-focused: "Cut onboarding time by half", not "Onboarding".

### Social Media

- **LinkedIn:** up to 1,300 characters. Open with a clear claim or a question in the first line. Use at most 3 hashtags at the end. Up to 2 emoji, only in informal posts.
- **Twitter/X:** up to 280 characters. Lead with the key fact. Use at most 2 hashtags. No emoji in B2B posts.

### Support / Help Content

- **Tone:** empathy first ("We know a failed payment is frustrating."), then the solution ("Here is how to fix it."), then a closing offer ("Reply if you need more help.").
- **Language:** partner with the reader. Use phrases such as "let's", "together", "we can".
- **Format:** numbered steps for procedures. Bold the names of UI elements, for example **Save**. End with an offer of more help.

## Grammar & Mechanics

- **Punctuation:** Oxford comma in lists. Use an em dash (—) without spaces for a short aside. Limit exclamation marks as described in SKILL.md.
- **Capitalization:** sentence case for headings. Capitalize product names as the product owner wrote them.
- **Numbers:** spell out numbers from one to nine. Use digits for 10 and above, for measurements, percentages, and times.
- **Inclusive language:** use "they" for people of unknown gender. Use "people" instead of "guys" or "manpower". Avoid "whitelist" and "blacklist", use "allowlist" and "blocklist".
